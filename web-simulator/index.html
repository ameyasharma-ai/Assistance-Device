【VERBATIM SOURCE TRANSCRIPTION】 Chapter 3: Laws of Motion

3.2 Newton's Second Law: Acceleration and Force.

Newton's Second Law describes the quantitative relationship among force, mass, and acceleration.

It states that the acceleration (a) of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting upon it and inversely proportional to its mass.

Formula: F = m · a, where F is the vector sum of forces in Newtons, m is the mass in kilograms, and a is acceleration in meters per second squared.

【KEY DEFINITIONS & LAWS】 Core Principles

Newton's Second Law: Net Force equals Mass multiplied by Acceleration (F = m · a).

Newton's Third Law: For every action in nature, there is an equal and opposite reaction force.

Action-Reaction Pairs: Two forces of equal magnitude acting on separate interacting bodies in opposite directions.

【CONCEPT EXPLANATIONS】 Physical Intuition

Think of pushing a heavy 25 kg box across a floor.

As your hands exert an action force pushing forward on the box, the box simultaneously exerts an identical reaction force backwards against your palms.

These forces never cancel out because they act on two completely different objects: one on the box, and one on your hands.

【WORKED SOLUTIONS】 Example 3.1 Step-by-Step

Problem: A 15 kg object is subjected to a net force of 45 Newtons. Calculate its acceleration.

Step 1: Identify known quantities. Mass m equals 15 kilograms. Force F equals 45 Newtons.

Step 2: Rearrange Newton's Second Law: Acceleration a equals Force divided by Mass.

Step 3: Calculate: 45 Newtons divided by 15 kilograms equals 3.0 meters per second squared.

【STUDY SUMMARY】 Key Takeaways

Higher force creates greater acceleration; higher mass dampens acceleration.

Action and reaction forces occur simultaneously and always in pairs.
